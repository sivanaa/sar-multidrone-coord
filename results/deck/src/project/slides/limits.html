Limits and next steps

What it doesn’t do yet

Limitations

CBBA favours the average person: the last one is reached 8–14% later than the best possible.

On a plain map, a lawnmower has the better worst case.

Thin margin in AirSim: closest 1.55 m against a 1.5 m floor.

Few live runs: one to six per setup, simulated drones only.

Future work

Min-max bids, so the last person is not left waiting.

Logical clocks for agreement between drones, then real hardware.

The lab’s models on a photoreal scene or real footage.

Repeated live runs to put error bars on flight results.

Be upfront about the limits. CBBA maximises the total value, which is essentially optimal for average waiting time, but it can leave the last person waiting longer; changing settings did not fix that, it needs a min-max objective. On an empty map, a lawnmower has the better worst case. The AirSim safety margin is thin. And the live evidence is one to six runs per configuration with simulated drones. Next steps follow directly: min-max bidding, logical timestamps instead of each drone’s clock before real hardware, running the lab’s models on realistic imagery, and repeated live runs.
